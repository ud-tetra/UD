Specified candidate interaction

Use the same oriented two-tetrahedron complex <0123,0124> and A=Bᵀ−B. Let Li be its skew incidence rotation between shared face 012 and cell i. Set A(g)=A+(g−1)(L1+L2). Binary g closes or opens both couplings; all other incidences remain unchanged. This is a candidate dynamic mask, motivated by the ledger's hard-gating structure, not a source-derived eligibility law. Removing only these coefficients does not create a new chain boundary: the masked boundary generally no longer squares to zero. The original topology and boundary remain fixed.

Switch-independent retained coordinates

q1=c0123,  q2=c0124,  f=c012
p1=c013−c023+c123
p2=c014−c024+c124
r=c01−c02+c12
z=(q1,q2,p1,p2,f,r).

This is an invertible change of coordinates from the previous record: p_i=v_i+f and r=w−q1−q2. Under a gate, the previous v and w remain defined reference aggregates; they are no longer the actual q and f derivatives. The new coordinates avoid that ambiguity.

q_i′ = p_i − g f
p_i′ = −3 q_i + r
f′   = r + g(q1+q2)
r′   = −p1−p2−3f.

The exact matrix identities R A(0)=H0 R and R(L1+L2)=D R prove R A(g)=(H0+gD)R for every real g, not just sampled binary settings. Separate gates gi also close: replace gf in the ith equation by gi f, and g(q1+q2) by g1 q1+g2 q2. The remaining equations are unchanged.

Switching and gate eligibility

For a prescribed piecewise-constant gate schedule with locally finite switching times, the quotient intertwines each exponential propagator and therefore their chronological product. The full state and z remain continuous at a switch; only the derivative changes. This audit assumes no impulsive reset. Arbitrary reset maps require their own compatibility check. The six continuous coordinates plus the currently held gate mode form the reduced hybrid state; externally prescribed decision times remain inputs.

Explicit candidate rule: at nominated decision times, set g=1 when −f(q1+q2)>0, otherwise set g=0, and hold that mode until the next decision. This uses the sign of the instantaneous shared-face contribution to total cell support, J=−2gf(q1+q2). The reduced record determines both eligibility and subsequent evolution exactly. The rule does not guarantee that J stays positive until the next decision, nor that total cell support grows: other faces also contribute. Decision times and the rule itself are assumptions, not UD-derived physical laws. No claim of absence of chattering is needed because decision times are stipulated to have no finite accumulation.

Exact hidden-eligibility counterexample

x+ = e0123+e012+e01−e12
x− = e0123+e012−e01+e12
R x+ = R x− = (1,0,0,0,1,0).

A different candidate rule g=1 when c01>0 chooses modes 1 and 0, respectively. The retained derivatives become (−1,−1,−3,0,1,−3) and (0,0,−3,0,0,−3). Thus the record is sufficient for the dynamics under a supplied mode but insufficient for this hidden-variable decision rule. This is not a no-go for all admissibility laws.

Invariant and ordering

The rows of R are orthogonal with squared norms (1,1,3,3,1,3). A common positive invariant for all gate settings is:

E = q1²+q2²+f²+(p1²+p2²+r²)/3.

E is the full squared norm of the orthogonal projection into the retained six-dimensional invariant sector, not generally the full 23-dimensional norm. Full norm is also conserved because A(g) remains skew. With continuous switching and no resets, E is continuous and constant across the entire schedule. This is a mathematical support invariant, not an identified physical energy. The closed and gate generators do not commute, so chronological ordering cannot be dropped.

Delta correction under feedback

If the baseline b and actual retained state z use the same supplied gate schedule, d=z−b obeys d′=H(g)d. If their states choose different gates, instead:

d′ = H(g_z)d + (g_z−g_b)D b.

The extra baseline-dependent term is required. Feedback-controlled deltas generally do not evolve in isolation. The record can remain compact and sufficient without being a homogeneous delta-only system.
