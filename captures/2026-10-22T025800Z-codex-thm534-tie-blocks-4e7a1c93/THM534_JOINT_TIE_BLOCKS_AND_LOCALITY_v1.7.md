## 2. Canonical connected tie partition and quotient orientation

At initialization, join each conflict edge \(\{f,g\}\) for which \(s_f=s_g\), and let \(\mathcal P\) be the connected components of this equal-stock subgraph. Each component has a common stock value \(s_C\). Isolated faces remain singleton blocks. These are the equivalence classes generated by equal-stock conflict edges: the partition makes precisely those merges forced by that equivalence relation. Using this relation as a physical event rule remains a candidate choice, not a consequence of conservation.

Define the quotient conflict graph \(Q\): blocks \(C\ne D\) are adjacent if some \(f\in C\) conflicts with some \(g\in D\). Their potential footprints are unions of the member footprints.

**Theorem T1.** For every initial stock assignment, the quotient admits the complete acyclic orientation

\[
C\longrightarrow D\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_C<s_D
\qquad (\{C,D\}\in E(Q)).
\]

The partition, quotient and orientation transform covariantly under signed carrier relabeling.

**Proof.** Equal-stock vertices connected by a path of equal-stock edges have the same stock, so each block is constant. Adjacent distinct blocks cannot have equal stock: their connecting conflict edge would have merged them. Thus every quotient edge has exactly one direction. A directed cycle would force a strictly increasing chain of stocks returning to its starting value. Relabeling preserves adjacency, equality, connectedness and order. Signed amplitude basis changes disappear on squaring, so the construction transports with the carrier. \(\square\)

The direction of priority is supplied. Reversing every initial quotient edge is another covariant acyclic choice. This theorem does not select that direction from UD sources.

## 3. Joint support map and exact receipts

For one retained block \(C\), define

\[
(M_C)_{f\tau}=\mathbf 1_{f\in C}D_{f\tau}h_{f\tau},\qquad
A_C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-M_C\\M_C^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here \(D\) is the frozen signed face–cell incidence matrix, and the incidence-specific contacts \(h_{f\tau}\) are supplied candidate inputs. They are not identified with a uniquely derived Q173 admission rule. Use the joint Cayley event

\[
x'=\left(I-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}A_C\right)^{-1}
       \left(I+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}A_C\right)x,
\qquad x=(z,t).
\tag{2}
\]

All nonparticipating coordinates remain unchanged. The control step is the earlier declared dimensionless \(\varepsilon=1/4\); its numerical value is not newly derived from a physical clock or Q161. The claims below hold for any real step. Lower-grade coordinates are held fixed in this face/core interaction family.

**Theorem T2.** Equation (2) exists, conserves support norm, and obeys completed midpoint face/core and equal-share owned-support receipts.

**Proof.** Since \(A_C^{\mathsf T}=-A_C\), the equation \((I-\varepsilon A_C/2)y=0\) implies \(\|y\|^2=0\) after taking its inner product with \(y\). Thus the inverse exists. With \(\bar z=(z+z')/2\), \(\bar t=(t+t')/2\), equation (2) gives

\[
z'-z=-\varepsilon M_C\bar t,\qquad
t'-t=\varepsilon M_C^{\mathsf T}\bar z.
\]

Set \(J_{f\tau}=2\varepsilon(M_C)_{f\tau}\bar z_f\bar t_\tau\). Multiplying each midpoint equation by twice its midpoint amplitude yields

\[
(z_f')^2-z_f^2=-\sum_\tau J_{f\tau},\qquad
(t_\tau')^2-t_\tau^2=\sum_fJ_{f\tau}.
\]

Summing proves support-norm conservation. For the retained equal-share face weights \(w_{f\tau}\), let \(S_\tau=t_\tau^2+\sum_fw_{f\tau}z_f^2\). Substitution gives

\[
\Delta S_\tau=\sum_f\left(J_{f\tau}-w_{f\tau}\sum_\nu J_{f\nu}\right).
\]

The weights sum to one over owners, so the owned-support changes sum to zero. This is the restricted face/core part of the ledger's ownership receipt; unchanged lower-grade support adds no change. No physical energy or action identification is made. \(\square\)

Equation (2) is the Cayley map of the sum of the member generators, not an ordered product of their separate Cayley maps. Those maps generally differ when their footprints overlap, as already established in v1.4. There is no calibrated duration equivalence between one joint event and several primitive events.

## 4. Frozen quotient scheduler

Freeze the initial partition and quotient, retain their orientation as scheduler state, and subsequently execute a nonempty subset of quotient sinks per batch, reversing their incident quotient edges after the attempts. Later stocks need not remain equal within a retained block. Repartitioning or recomputing priority would be a different algorithm and has no fairness certificate here.

**Theorem T3.** On a finite connected \(G\), the frozen quotient scheduler is free of scheduling deadlock, its batched joint maps commute, and block \(C\) receives an attempt within

\[
B_C=1+\sum_{D\in\mathcal P}d_Q(C,D)
\]

individual **block attempts**, starting at any prefix.

**Proof.** Contracting connected blocks preserves connectedness, so \(Q\) is connected. Quotient sinks are independent, hence their union footprints are disjoint. The local joint maps and their mask evaluations therefore commute. A finite acyclic orientation has a sink; sink reversal makes that vertex a source and cannot create a directed cycle. A batch sequentializes because its sinks are independent. These facts reproduce v1.5 on \(Q\).

For an initially oriented quotient edge \(C\to D\), its endpoints alternate attempted service starting with \(D\); other events cannot reverse their edge. Thus \(0\le N_D-N_C\le1\). Along a shortest path, \(|N_D-N_C|\le d_Q(C,D)\). If \(N_C=0\), at most \(\sum_Dd_Q(C,D)\) block attempts can have occurred. The proof restarts at every prefix because acyclicity persists. \(\square\)

Every member face participates in a block's joint attempt, but this does not assert a nonzero current, successful reset or individual primitive-face service count. All-off masks remain identity maps. Edge reversal proceeds even when a joint support event is an identity. A source rule refusing the attempt or reversal can invalidate this theorem's execution hypothesis.

## 6. Costs and counterexamples

**No uniform locality bound.** If all face stocks agree on a connected conflict graph, the partition has one block spanning the entire graph. On \(K_-\), its event includes seven face and two core coordinates. On connected carriers of increasing size, its footprint and graph diameter can grow without bound. Computing the whole equal-stock component also need not be a bounded-radius operation. Thus grouping ties resolves a label-choice problem by enlarging the event; it has not recovered a uniformly local single-face dynamics or a spacetime causal law.

**Nonzero stationary state with open contacts.** Set \(z_{013}=z_{023}=1\), all other face and both core amplitudes zero. The retained blocks are \(\{013,023\}\) and the connected complement. Their incidence contributions into cell \(0123\) cancel because \(D_{013,0}=1\), \(D_{023,0}=-1\). Every joint block generator annihilates this state, even with all contacts open. Every scheduled support event is therefore an identity. Fair block attempts do not imply support activation or recovery. This example has support norm squared 2 and is the same nonzero signed-symmetry control used in v1.6.

**Discontinuity at an exact tie.** Perturb only \(z_{023}\) to \(1+\delta\), \(\delta>0\). The two positive-stock faces become separate blocks, and the first quotient sink is the singleton \(023\). With all contacts open and \(\varepsilon=1/4\), its first update is

\[
t_0'=-\frac{16}{65}(1+\delta),\qquad
z_{023}'=\frac{63}{65}(1+\delta).
\]

As \(\delta\downarrow0\), the first updated core approaches \(-16/65\), whereas it is exactly zero at the tie under the joint event. The first-block state-update map is therefore discontinuous. This compares one attempt in each declared event family; it does not compare calibrated physical durations. The exact-tie construction is not a robust physical tie detector. Adding a tolerance, soft grouping or asynchronous negotiation would introduce a new rule and require a new safety/fairness/receipt audit.
