## 1. Objects and declared assumptions

Fix an even integer \(N\ge4\) and \(L=2\). Let \(X_N\) be the **Freudenthal tetrahedralization** of \(Q_N\times[0,L]\), where \(Q_N=[0,N]^2\) is triangulated by the southwest-to-northeast diagonal in each grid square. Tetrahedra are 3-simplices with the combinatorics of \(K_4\). Let \(v=(N/2,N/2)\) be the selected interior planar vertex and \(q_j=(v,j)\) at levels \(j=0,1,2\).

The *global source state* is the product of typed spaces
\[
\mathcal S=\{\text{supported tetrahedral subcomplexes of }X_N\}\times
\bigl(V(X_N)\cup\{\bot\}\bigr)\times 2^{V(X_N)}\times\{0,1\}.
\]
Write a state \((K,b,M,s)\) for the active supported 3-complex \(K\), unit-token location \(b\), persistent trail-memory set \(M\), and boundary-reservoir occupancy \(s\). Define \(K[M]\) to be the subcomplex generated by all tetrahedra of the original \(X_N\) avoiding \(M\); unsupported subfaces are pruned. The discrete event index is not physical time.

**NEW ASSUMPTIONS:** (E1) a unit token is seeded at \(q_0\); (E2) every occupied vertex has the local **+z port** where an incident supported edge exists; (E3) the token moves *before* its vacated vertex enters trail-memory and its tetrahedral star is excised; (E4) a separate persistent memory field retains marks even after corresponding geometric vertices disappear; (E5) the upper boundary can transfer the token to an explicitly typed sink, and no cell restoration is allowed while marks persist. These rules are constructive model assumptions, **not** recovered from the frozen K4 action, burden or parity dynamics. There is no global precomputed path list in the transition operator.

## 2. The local transition rule

Initialize \(K=K[\varnothing]=X_N\), \(b=q_0\), \(M=\varnothing\), \(s=0\). For event \(j<L\):

1. From the **current** occupied site \(b=(v,j)\), inspect its +z port to \(b'=(v,j+1)\); require the edge \(\{b,b'\}\) to be supported by at least one live tetrahedron.
2. Transfer the unit token to \(b'\) across this live edge.
3. Mark only the **vacated** site, \(M'=M\cup\{b\}\), then update local occupancy to \(K'=K[M']\). Leave \(s\) unchanged.

At the upper boundary transfer \(b=q_L\) to the sink: set \(b'=\bot\), \(s'=1\), mark \(q_L\) and replace \(K\) by \(K[M\cup\{q_L\}]\). The sink is a separate boundary source/stock object; it is **not** an extant K4 vertex or physical apparatus.

## 3. Theorem (local liveness, conserved content, final topology)

For every even \(N\ge4\), the evolution is well-defined throughout; no path beyond the current +z neighbor is needed. It satisfies
\[
\mathbf1\{b\neq\bot\}+s=1
\]
under every update. Its completed geometric state has
\[
|K_{\mathrm{final}}|\cong\bigl(Q_N\setminus \operatorname{ost}(v)\bigr)\times[0,2],
\qquad H_1(K_{\mathrm{final}};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
while \(H_1(X_N;\mathbb Z)=0\). Every update is confined to the star of its current vertex and a single neighboring port; the number of affected K4 cells is bounded uniformly in \(N\). If, after the final event, the geometry is held fixed or only PL-homeomorphism-preserving moves are permitted, then the nontrivial first homology persists. Retained trail-memory prohibits **refilling the marked column**, but does not by itself prevent an additional topology-changing deletion elsewhere from destroying the tunnel. This is **kinematic persistence under a restricted post-event generator**, not energetic or dynamical attraction.

### Proof

Every monotone grid vertex has a supported +z edge in a tetrahedron of the slab immediately above it, as it is a vertical edge of at least one Freudenthal tetrahedron. At event \(j<L\), all already marked vertices have levels strictly below \(j\). Therefore tetrahedra in the untouched slab \([j,j+1]\) are disjoint from \(M\). In particular, the +z edge from the current vertex is still a face of a live tetrahedron. The movement occurs **before** its supporting old star is removed. By induction the transition is enabled for \(j=0,1\). Only the just-vacated star is pruned; local Freudenthal vertex degrees are bounded (at most 8 adjacent cubes, each containing six tetrahedra) independently of \(N\). No global itinerary is read by the rule.

An interior transfer removes one token occupancy and creates one at the neighbor, preserving the indicator plus sink. At the final boundary event token occupancy falls by one as sink occupancy increases by one, so the sum remains one.

After the final event the marked set is exactly \(\{(v,0),(v,1),(v,2)\}\). For each triangular base face containing \(v\), every tetrahedron in its prism has a copy of \(v\) at the lower or upper endpoint and is removed. No tetrahedron over a base face avoiding \(v\) is removed. Hence the supported complex triangulates the product of the punctured planar disk (the six-triangle open star has been excised) and the interval. This product deformation retracts onto a circle, with integral homology \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z\), while the original disk times interval is contractible. If subsequent updates leave the supported complex fixed, its homology is unchanged. More generally, legal PL-homeomorphism-preserving face moves preserve its integral homology. The retained memory prohibits refilling the marked column, but does not rule out new topology-changing deletion elsewhere. QED.

## 4. Independent finite checks and sharpness

`verify_transfer_and_scaling.py` uses an independent six-permutation tetrahedralization and full \(\mathbb F_2\) chain ranks at \(N=4,6,8,10\). At \(N=4,6,8\) its tetrahedral counts are respectively:

| \(N\) | Initial | After transfer 1 | After transfer 2 | Final sink/excision | Final \(\beta_1(\mathbb F_2)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 192 | 180 | 162 | 156 | 1 |
| 6 | 432 | 420 | 402 | 396 | 1 |
| 8 | 768 | 756 | 738 | 732 | 1 |
| 10 | 1200 | 1188 | 1170 | 1164 | 1 |

**Additional numerical observation:** \(\beta_1(\mathbb F_2)=1\) already after the second transfer in these tested complexes. This earlier intermediate transition is **not** promoted to a general integral theorem by finite examples; only the final state has the separate all-\(N\) integral proof above.

**NO-GO retained:** Reversing the order (excision first, then move using the surviving graph) removes the token vertex and all incident live edges, blocking any move, exactly as PRED04-CAUSAL-003 proved. Removing the memory/restoration veto can immediately erase the final tunnel by reinserting original K4 tetrahedra. Conversely, even with retained memory, removing a radial strip connecting the inner and outer annular boundaries changes the annulus into a simply connected planar region and can eliminate its nontrivial first homology: **persistent memory alone is not a complete topological protection theorem**. **Seed and port are still exogenous**, so this is a locally executable rule and not a proof of spontaneous homogeneous K4 symmetry breaking.
