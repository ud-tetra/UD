The tetrahedral carrier

A filled tetrahedron contains four vertices, six edges, four triangular faces, and one three-dimensional cell. The paper uses this finite object as a relational carrier; it does not identify the tetrahedron with physical space.

02 · Algebraic topology

Geometry becomes a chain complex

The tetrahedron is reorganized by simplex dimension. Boundary maps carry a volume to faces, faces to edges, and edges to vertices. Applying two consecutive boundary maps gives zero: internal orientations cancel exactly.

Incidence carries the flow

A chain state assigns an amplitude to every simplex. The skew Hodge–Dirac generator exchanges amplitude only across incidences—vertex to edge, edge to face, and face to cell—while the total quadratic support remains constant.

04 · Binary fiber

Eight addresses, four Hamming shells

One binary coordinate is assigned to each opposite-edge class. The eight three-bit addresses form a cube. Grouping them by the number of active bits produces the exact multiplicities (1,3,3,1).

05 · Retriangulation

Change the interior, preserve the boundary

A 2↔3 Pachner move replaces two tetrahedra sharing an internal face with three tetrahedra sharing an internal edge. The outer triangulated boundary is unchanged while the internal decomposition changes.

Why a Projective Support Receipt is needed

Simple support summaries do not identify the chain ray. A sufficient PSR adds the information needed to propagate relative signs across each connected support component.

Reconstruct on fourteen; test on fourteen

The fully supported filled tetrahedron has twenty-eight Hasse incidences. The protocol fixes an intrinsic split: fourteen incidences reconstruct the state and fourteen remain held out for a genuine prediction test.
