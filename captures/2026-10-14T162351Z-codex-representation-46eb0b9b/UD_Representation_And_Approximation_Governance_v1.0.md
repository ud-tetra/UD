# UD core methodological governance: representation and approximation

14 October 2026 · Version 1.0

**Authority:** user-authorized methodological addition following the representation assessment and instruction to continue. Applies to ongoing UD development. Repository incorporation is recorded separately through its pull request. This is a methodological rule, not a theorem-confidence or physical-promotion receipt. Physical promotion: 0.

## Core principle

Keep mathematical objects, their encodings, observations, and physical interpretations separately typed. Preserve exact expressions where available. Introduce numerical approximations with declared precision and error bounds. Equivalent representations must give consistent predictions under the corresponding transformations. Coarse-graining, projection, and truncation must identify what information they discard. Properties of notation, including numeral base and digit patterns, do not constitute evidence of physical structure.

## R01. Object and representation

Name the underlying object and the representation used. Distinguish a state from coordinates, a quantity from its numeral, a physical observable from an algebraic proxy, and a mathematical carrier from its physical interpretation. A real chain coordinate, a binary matching label, a quantum apparatus state and a retained receipt must not be substituted for one another without an explicit map.

## R02. Exact-first arithmetic

Keep integers, reduced fractions, radicals, defining polynomials, exact matrix certificates and other applicable exact specifications through derivations. Use decimals for presentation or controlled numerical work. Fraction-first does not mean every quantity is rational: sqrt(10) is irrational and is exactly specified by its positive-root definition. Exact notation does not validate the physical model that uses it.

## R03. Approximation ownership

For an operative approximation, record the source object, method, precision, norm or error metric, domain, and certified error where available. Separate display rounding from replacing a quantity in the dynamics. Label an uncertified numerical estimate as such. Avoid inferring exact zero, degeneracy, recurrence, positivity or equality from rounded numbers. A tolerance chosen after viewing an endpoint is not a source law.

## R04. Equivalent representations and covariance

For an invertible coordinate change y=T x, transport the generator, metric, observation, noise and unit conventions consistently. Predictions for corresponding observables must agree. If the original flow is x'=A x, the equivalent generator is T A T^{-1}; the norm metric becomes (T^{-1})^T H T^{-1}. Replacing the generator or retaining the wrong metric changes the model rather than merely its notation. Gauge equivalence requires a declared equivalence relation and observable map.

## R05. Information-losing maps

Projection, rounding, marginalization and coarse-graining are generally not invertible changes of representation. State the information they forget and the conditions under which the retained description closes. Do not identify observation loss with full-state erasure, physical entropy production or irreversible record formation. A reduced state may need signed residual coordinates, derivative memory, reference data or a joint error model.

## R06. Precision and apparatus resources

Mathematical exactness is not a certificate of unlimited physical readout precision. Declare resolution, acquisition uncertainty, references, backaction and preparation where operational claims depend on them. If a richer observation improves distinguishability, include its added channels and resource demands. Do not assume exact derivative measurements are free or automatically available.

## R07. Representation neutrality of physical claims

Numeral bases are encoding choices. Nonterminating or nonperiodic digits do not establish fractality, randomness, scale invariance, continuity or discreteness of nature. Claims about those properties require object-level definitions and derivations. An irrational number has a nonterminating, non-eventually-periodic positional expansion in every integer base b>=2; an exact infinite expansion need not lose information.

## R08. Mathematical closure and physical identification

An exact theorem is conditional on its declared objects and premises. Its physical identification requires a separate justified observable correspondence and evidence. Repeated citation, numerical coincidence and a change of notation cannot improve bridge status. Retain source provenance, no target backsolving, failure branches and existing review/promotion rules. This addition does not change any confidence rung.

## Required claim annotation

For each consequential numerical or representation-dependent claim, provide the relevant fields:

| Field | Required content |
|:--|:--|
| Object | State, operator, count, observation or physical quantity |
| Encoding | Basis, orientation, metric, numeral convention and units as applicable |
| Exact specification | Formula, defining relation or exact certificate where available |
| Approximation | Method, precision and validity domain if used |
| Error | Certified bound, or explicitly uncertified estimate |
| Information loss | Projection, discarded correlations or coarse-graining |
| Physical bridge | Declared, derived, measured or OPEN |

Not every prose sentence needs a full table. Attach these fields to the claim or its source record; use the subset that applies.

## Application to the narrative manuscript

The two-cell example separates preserved full-state distance from cell-only readout loss. The 30-to-23 interface becomes reversible only when its seven residual coordinates are retained. Gaussian readout results depend on the stipulated encoding and covariance. Entropy and event-time claims require their own coarse-graining and physical maps. These are concrete applications of the principle.

A useful recurrence example concerns the exact frequencies sqrt(3), sqrt(5). Replacing them in the model by 1.732=433/250 and 2.236=559/250 creates a common period 500 pi in that rounded-frequency model, whereas the exact pair has no positive common period. Merely displaying those decimals while retaining exact computation does not create this artefact. The example illustrates why approximation must be owned.
