The Big Idea

What is Unified Dynamics?

For centuries, scientists have had separate "rule books" for each part of nature — one book for gravity, another for atoms, another for electricity. Unified Dynamics proposes there is only one underlying rule book, and everything else is a consequence.

🏗️
Think of it like LEGO. There's one type of brick. With it, you can build a house, a spaceship, a dragon, a city. You don't need different types of bricks for different things — the variety comes from how you connect them. Unified Dynamics says the universe works the same way: one fundamental "brick" (a distinction, a difference), one rule for connecting them, and all of nature emerges from that.
🎵
Or think of music. Every song — a lullaby, a symphony, heavy metal — is made from the same 12 notes and the same physics of sound waves. The richness comes from how you arrange them. UD says all the richness of the universe comes from arranging one kind of fundamental relationship.
The one-sentence version
"You can't have something without something else to compare it to — and that simple fact, followed to its logical conclusion, generates all of physics."

That's it. Everything flows from the logic of distinction: the idea that A can only exist if it's different from not-A.

Why does this matter?

If UD is right, scientists no longer need to guess at new "ingredients" for each phenomenon. Instead of 19 unexplained numbers in the Standard Model of physics, UD derives them from zero free parameters — just logic and geometry.

Step by Step

How does it actually work?

UD builds the universe from scratch, one logical step at a time. Here's the journey from absolute nothing to atoms, gravity, and life:

1

Start with the simplest possible thing

The most basic thing that can exist is a distinction — a difference between "something" and "not something." This isn't borrowed from physics; it's pure logic.

💡
Everyday example: "Dark" only exists because "light" exists. "Hot" only makes sense because "cold" exists. You can't have one without the other. That's a distinction.
\( A \neq \bar{A} \)
A is different from not-A. Something exists by being distinct from its opposite.
2

Distinctions force relationships

Once you have two different things, they must relate to each other. A distinction cannot float in isolation — it only exists through contrast. This forces the universe to be fundamentally relational, not made of isolated particles.

🤝
Everyday example: "Taller than" only makes sense between two people. Relationships aren't added to things — they're built in from the start.
3

The simplest structure that fits: the tetrahedron

When you work out the math rigorously — what's the minimal structure that can hold distinctions AND their relationships AND remain self-consistent? — the answer is a tetrahedron: four points, six edges, four triangular faces.

🏔️
Why a tetrahedron specifically? It's the simplest 3D shape where every point connects to every other point. Think of it as the "most democratic" shape — no vertex is privileged, every relationship is represented. It's the minimal self-consistent unit of reality.
4

Stack tetrahedra infinitely → get space, time, and matter

Tile infinite tetrahedra together using a mathematical pattern called an IFS (Iterated Function System) — think of how a snowflake grows by repeating the same pattern at every scale — and space, time, particles, and forces all emerge naturally.

❄️
Snowflake analogy: A snowflake looks completely different at different zoom levels — yet it's all made from the same hexagonal rule, repeated. UD's "quasicrystal" spacetime is similar: zoom in and zoom out, the same tetrahedral pattern repeats. Particles are places where this pattern has a "defect" — a knot or glitch in the tiling.
\( \Delta/N = 0.0361 \)
One number — the "overlap deficit" of how tetrahedra tile — controls 10 major constants of physics. It's like tuning one dial and getting the entire universe.

Common Questions

Your questions, answered

Unified Dynamics is ambitious. Here are honest answers to the most common questions — including the skeptical ones.

Isn't "a theory of everything" usually crackpot science? ›
Fair skepticism! The key difference is that UD makes specific, falsifiable, quantitative predictions that match experiment — and it does so with zero free parameters. Crackpot theories typically have no equations, or have equations that can match anything. UD's predictions are pre-committed: if the universe disagrees with any one of them, the whole theory fails. That's what makes it scientific.
Has it been peer reviewed yet? ›
Not yet — but this is the explicit goal. The theory is currently in the final stages of preparation for submission. This website is part of making the work transparent and accessible. An honest theory presents itself openly so anyone can examine and critique it before formal review.
What's a "tetrahedron" and why does it matter so much? ›
A tetrahedron is the simplest solid shape — four triangular faces, four vertices, six edges. Think of a pyramid with a triangular base. It's the only shape where every vertex connects to every other vertex in 3D space, making it uniquely "democratic." UD shows that when you rigorously ask "what's the minimal consistent relational structure?" — the tetrahedron is the mathematical answer. From there, tiling space with tetrahedra (a quasicrystal) produces all of physics as an emergent consequence.
Does UD replace Einstein or quantum mechanics? ›
No — it derives them. Einstein's equations and quantum mechanics are both outputs of UD, not things it competes with. It's like saying calculus "replaces" arithmetic — it doesn't; it builds on it and extends it. UD shows why Einstein and quantum mechanics are both right, and gives a single framework that unifies them (which Einstein and later physicists never managed to do).
What's the Clay Millennium Prize connection? ›
The Clay Mathematics Institute offers $1 million for solving 7 great math problems. One — the Yang-Mills mass gap — asks: why do the particles that carry the strong nuclear force have mass? UD provides an almost-complete answer: 11 of 12 proof steps are rigorous. The one remaining step has been precisely identified. UD does not claim the prize is solved — but it has made a concrete contribution to the approach.
What about consciousness? Does UD explain the mind? ›
UD includes one explicitly conjectural idea (labeled T40) suggesting consciousness might be connected to sufficiently recursive, self-referential closed motifs. This is marked as speculative and does not affect any other result in the theory. It's an interesting hypothesis — not a claim. The rest of UD stands entirely independently of it.
Is this the same as string theory or loop quantum gravity? ›
No, though it lives in the same neighborhood. String theory adds extra dimensions and strings as fundamental objects. Loop quantum gravity quantizes spacetime directly. UD doesn't add anything — it starts from pure logical necessity (distinction → relation → tetrahedral structure) and derives spacetime and particles as emergent consequences. One notable difference: UD predicts minimal area = ℓ_P² (Planck length squared) with no free parameter; loop quantum gravity requires an unexplained "Immirzi parameter" to get this (~5.17 ℓ_P²).
