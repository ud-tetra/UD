# Unified Dynamics: how change produces things that last

Consolidated narrative revision 0.2 · 14 October 2026

Development — no registry entry. Physical promotion: 0. This is an editorial synthesis of declared finite models and research aims, not a new physical derivation.

## Public narrative

The world changes, yet it contains things we can recognize again. A signal travels. A structure survives an encounter. An interaction leaves a trace. Unified Dynamics begins with a question connecting these familiar experiences: how can changing states produce persistent structures and readable records?

UD seeks a common account of three processes: formation, persistence, and observation. Its motivation is to understand their connections. A structure must be produced before it can endure, and its continued existence does not guarantee that its state is accessible to a particular readout. A physical account must explain the mechanisms linking those steps.

### Why begin with distinctions?

Before a model can explain change, it must specify what can differ. UD begins with distinctions: differences between declared states that a specified description or readout can resolve. This is a way of making the basic question precise. What are the alternatives? Which interactions alter them? Which features remain recognizable afterward?

A distinction is not automatically a bit stored somewhere, and a state need not be entirely discrete. The finite models can include real or complex amplitudes and phases. The term identifies a difference that matters within the declared model; it does not by itself establish a complete theory of existence or measurement.

### Why introduce geometry?

A list of possible states does not tell us which parts can interact. Geometry supplies relationships: adjacency, boundaries, orientation, and the assembly of parts into a whole.

UD uses tetrahedral packet complexes as a candidate architecture for those relationships. A tetrahedron contains vertices, edges, triangular faces, and a three-dimensional cell with a closed boundary. These levels provide a compact setting for studying motion between parts, exchange across interfaces, and coupling between cells.

This is a practical mathematical reason to investigate tetrahedra. It makes interactions explicit and calculations inspectable. Establishing why this architecture should describe nature, whether it is unique, or how physical dimensionality follows remains further work. The narrative should explain the candidate's role before making those larger arguments.

### Why do shapes matter?

Changing the connections can change the dynamics. Two cells sharing a face and three cells sharing an edge support different mode structures under the declared chain flow. Shape therefore has a functional meaning: it can affect which patterns move, recur, or remain accessible.

The model must calculate that function. A visual resemblance or a repeated number does not determine it. Comparisons need explicit preparations, interactions, readouts, and resource constraints so that the effect of the architecture can be assessed.

### Why distinguish persistence from formation?

The behaviour of an existing structure does not explain how it came into being. A fixed complex supports calculations of transport and modes. Assembly requires an additional rule for changing the complex—through attachment, separation, replacement, or reconstruction—and a map for transferring the state through that change.

Persistence asks another question: after formation, which properties survive interaction? A configuration may be easy to produce and short-lived, or difficult to produce and durable. Growth requires both an available formation pathway and survival of its output.

This gives the intuition of coherent agreement a specific research role. Compatibility between phases or boundary states may affect an interaction. To explain stabilization or clustering, the dynamics must show how that compatibility changes attachment, leakage, survival, or growth.

### Why does observation need its own account?

A small example makes the problem concrete. Prepare an excitation in either of two tetrahedra joined along a face. Under the declared coherent evolution, the full states remain distinct. Yet at particular times their cell amplitudes are identical. An observer reading only those amplitudes cannot determine where the excitation began, although other state coordinates still distinguish the alternatives.

The lesson is that preserving a difference and making it available to a chosen readout are different achievements. The complete system can retain a distinction while a restricted observation loses access to it.

### Why add memory and resource accounting?

A record requires an interaction that places alternatives into states which remain readable for a specified duration. A write mechanism, a retention rule, and an error criterion must therefore be supplied.

Repeated record formation also requires resources. Blank memory, control, energy, readout, and environmental renewal have to appear in the account. These requirements explain why UD's memory work examines joint error states and complete cost rather than treating a passing signal as an irreversible record.

Entropy belongs here through an explicit state and coarse-graining rule. The entropy of a memory by itself need not measure how much information it retains about its source. Any proposed thermodynamic or geometric entropy law needs the additional derivation appropriate to that claim.

### Why count events?

Counting makes activity auditable. It allows histories to be compared, operations to be tracked, and resource demands to be related to outcomes. A discrete update or scheduling event gives a defined unit of model activity.

Connecting that unit to a physical clock requires a further map. Event counts, coherent evolution parameters, retained history, and proper time serve different roles until their relationships have been derived.

### What would make this a physical theory?

UD's larger ambition is to connect the finite mechanisms to physical observables. That requires controlled descriptions at larger scales, physical units and calibrations, and results that distinguish the proposed account from alternatives.

A spectrum can establish the behaviour of a declared interaction. A retention calculation can establish the performance of a declared memory. Deriving spacetime geometry, matter identities, or gravitational normalization demands additional bridges. The programme's credibility rests on making those bridges explicit and showing where they succeed or fail.

UD is therefore organized around a causal progression: interactions generate and transform states; some configurations retain identifiable properties; some interactions preserve accessible records. Tetrahedral complexes provide a candidate setting in which to investigate whether these processes can be connected within a common account of physical dynamics.

## Compact public introduction

Unified Dynamics investigates how constrained interactions can produce persistent structures and readable records. It uses tetrahedral packet complexes as a candidate architecture for studying how states move, how arrangements affect their behaviour, and what mechanisms retain distinguishable information. Its broader aim is to connect formation, persistence, and observation to physical laws through explicit derivations. Exact finite results and proposed physical bridges remain separately identified.
