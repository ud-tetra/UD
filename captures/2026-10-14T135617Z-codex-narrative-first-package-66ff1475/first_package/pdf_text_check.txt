Abstract
Unified Dynamics investigates whether the formation of structures, their persistence through in-
teraction, and the production of readable records can be connected within a common dynamical
account. This manuscript develops that question through a narrative synthesis of four source
papers. It begins with declared distinctions and tetrahedral chain carriers, derives coherent in-
cidence exchange and its symmetry restrictions, and shows why preserved full-state distinctions
need not remain accessible to restricted observations. It then treats projective reconstruction
as an information-access problem and finite capture and renewal as explicit apparatus construc-
tions. Geometry determines possible relationships; dynamics determines their consequences;
reconstruction identifies the information a readout must supply; and record formation requires
an additional retention mechanism. Exact finite results are separated from supplied coeﬀicients,
prepared resources, configuration changes and physical interpretation. The resulting account
provides a coherent theoretical foundation and identifies the mechanisms still needed for native
formation, complete autonomous renewal and a calibrated spacetime description. It does not
derive a physical metric, a universal entropy-area normalization, or a gravitational field equation.
Keywords: tetrahedral complexes; incidence dynamics; persistence; projective reconstruction;
finite memory; capture; renewal.

1. The problem: how does change produce things that last?
The world changes, yet it contains things we can recognize again. Signals travel, structures
survive encounters, and interactions leave traces. A theoretical account should explain how
these processes connect: how a structure is generated, how it preserves identifiable properties,
and how its behaviour becomes available to another process.
These are related questions with different mathematical requirements. Existence in a state space
is not a formation mechanism. Conservation of a full-state quantity is not guaranteed access
through a restricted readout. A successful transfer is not permanent memory. The purpose of
this manuscript is to make those distinctions part of one explanatory argument.
The organizing question is:
     Can constrained interactions explain the formation of persistent structures and read-
     able records within a common dynamical framework?
UD investigates tetrahedral packet complexes as a candidate architecture for that framework.
The word packet denotes a declared carrier and its state; it does not independently establish a
particle, a material substance or an isolated unit of physical energy. The theory must earn each
of those interpretations through a further map to observables.
The approach begins with a finite setting because its relationships and consequences can be
inspected exactly. A small carrier permits an explicit boundary matrix, an exact spectrum, a
complete observation map and a finite apparatus state. Larger claims should remain accountable
to these constructions.
Our narrative progresses through five questions. What can differ? What can interact? What
survives that interaction? What can a readout recover? What mechanism preserves the recov-
ered difference as a record? Formation and the physical bridge then become precise obligations
rather than unexplained transitions between equations.


2. Distinction: specify the alternatives before explaining their
evolution
2.1 State, observation and resolvability
Let a declared state space be 𝒳 and an observation map be 𝑂 ∶ 𝒳 → 𝒴. Two states 𝑥, 𝑦 are dis-
tinguishable under this exact observation when 𝑂(𝑥) ≠ 𝑂(𝑦). Under a noisy or finite-resolution
observation, distinguishability additionally depends on the output distributions, permitted de-
cisions and error criterion.
This operational definition makes the model’s alternatives explicit. It does not imply that phys-
ical existence depends on observation, nor that every state difference is already an irreversible
record. Distinction under a readout is relative to a specified map.
For real chain states, the observation might retain signed coordinates or only their squares.
For complex states, it might retain amplitudes, phases or bilinear receipts. These alternatives
preserve different information. A narrative that calls them all “information” without naming
the map would conceal the central problem.

3. Why tetrahedral structure enters
3.1 Relationships before metric
A state list does not determine which parts interact. A simplicial complex makes those rela-
tionships explicit through containment and shared boundaries. A filled tetrahedron includes
vertices, edges, faces and a cell. Its chain dimensions are


                            𝑓(Δ3 ) = (4, 6, 4, 1),       dim 𝒞(Δ3 ) = 15.

It is the smallest three-dimensional simplex. This supplies a compact architecture with a closed
two-dimensional boundary and several incidence levels. It provides a tractable setting for ex-
change within a cell and coupling between cells.
This explains why it is useful to investigate tetrahedra. It does not prove uniqueness among
candidate architectures or derive the dimension of physical space. A topological cell dimension,
a chain-register dimension and an apparatus Hilbert-space dimension are distinct objects.

6.3 Equal exterior boundary is insuﬀicient for dynamical equivalence
The oriented 2-to-3 move has equal exterior boundary because its difference is the boundary
of [01234]. Its two sides nevertheless have different carrier dimensions and spectra. A norm-
preserving coordinate inclusion need not intertwine their continuous flows.
The source also gives a concise incompatibility witness. Boundary fixation and chain compati-
bility force a top chain of squared norm 2 to map to a filling of squared norm 3. An isometry
cannot implement that forced image [1, §6.5]. Thus boundary preservation, chain compatibility
and Euclidean isometry cannot all be imposed under that linear contract.
Geometry creation needs its own state-transfer law. Equality of boundary chains does not select
it.

7. Persistence and formation
7.1 State preservation does not imply attraction
Under the fixed skew flow, different initial states keep their separation. In particular, a state
cannot converge to a different fixed point: its distance from that point remains constant. Dis-
tance to an invariant linear subspace is also preserved, because orthogonal evolution preserves
both the subspace and its orthogonal complement.
This permits persistent modes and recurrence. It excludes attracting convergence to those tar-
gets in that particular full-state model. A projected observable can show apparent concentration
without establishing full-state attraction. Open or nonlinear dynamics requires a separate anal-
ysis.

7.2 An explicit prepared growth construction
The source contains a useful assembly rule [3, §11.2]: begin with one tetrahedron and attach
each new cell to a boundary face using a fresh vertex. Each step adds one vertex, three edges,
three faces and one cell. It removes one boundary face and creates three. Therefore after 𝑚
cells,

assembly grammar without a unique shape selector.

faces—is a member of this class when all four attachments use distinct fresh vertices. It has

7.3 What a preference claim must specify
A structure may form rarely and survive well, or form frequently and fail quickly. Its occur-
rence depends on both processes. Comparing candidates requires a common initial ensemble,
configuration-changing rules, state transfers, disturbances and resources.
A persistence criterion should name the retained property, tolerance and duration. Possible prop-
erties include bounded leakage, retained distinguishability or valid receipt consistency. These
are different criteria. Coherent agreement explains selection only when a stated interaction
makes that agreement alter formation or survival.
The source’s four-coordinate averaging law provides a warning [3, §12.1]:


                                  𝑇 (𝑠) = 𝑃0 + 𝑒−4𝑠/3 (𝐼 − 𝑃0 ).

It preserves the uniform component but suppresses contrast. Retaining total stock through av-
eraging does not establish a localized bound structure. Formation, localization and information
retention must remain separately tested.
