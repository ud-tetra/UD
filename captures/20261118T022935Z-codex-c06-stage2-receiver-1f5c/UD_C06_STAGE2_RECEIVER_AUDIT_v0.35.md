## Receiver obstruction

The Klein four subgroup \(V_4\subset S_4\) consists of the identity and the three double transpositions. It fixes each perfect matching as an unordered pair of edges and hence acts trivially on all of \(M\). On the odd edge basis \((01-23,02-13,03-12)\), the three nonidentity elements preserve one coordinate and reverse the other two. These actions have no nonzero common fixed vector. For a linear equivariant transfer \(L:T_2\to M\), averaging \(\rho(g)\) over \(V_4\) gives zero on \(T_2\). Since \(L\rho(g)=L\) for each \(g\), this gives \(L=0\).

More generally, any map \(F:T_2\to M\) equivariant under this same action satisfies \(F(gw)=F(w)\). Every nonzero \(w\) has a distinct point in its \(V_4\) orbit. Hence no such map is injective on the held family, even if nonlinear. The same conclusion holds for a map of the aligned pair \((a,a)\) and an already populated matching state, because \(V_4\) fixes the latter. The obstruction concerns the matching register alone under ordinary relabeling. A frame, separately retained symmetry-sensitive data, or a receiver carrying a \(T_2\) action changes the premises.
