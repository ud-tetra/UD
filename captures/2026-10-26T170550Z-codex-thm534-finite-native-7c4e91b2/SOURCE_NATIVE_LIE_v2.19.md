## 2. Native invariant split

The exact boundary ranks are \(\operatorname{rank}B_1=4\), \(\operatorname{rank}B_2=5\), \(\operatorname{rank}B_3=2\), with \(B_1B_2=B_2B_3=0\). Thus

\[
\mathcal C=\ker B_1=\operatorname{im}B_2\subset C_1,
\qquad \dim\mathcal C=5.
\]

Define the orthogonal native sectors

\[
\mathcal A=\mathcal C\oplus C_2\oplus C_3,
\quad \dim\mathcal A=14,
\qquad
\mathcal G=C_0\oplus\operatorname{im}B_1^{\mathsf T},
\quad \dim\mathcal G=9.
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem L1 (common invariant sectors).** Every \(A_h\) preserves both sectors. On \(\mathcal G\), every mask has the same generator

\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&-B_1\\ B_1^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},
\tag{3}
\]

restricted to \(C_0\oplus\operatorname{im}B_1^{\mathsf T}\). On \(\mathcal A\), the face controls and the cycle–face part of the drift act nontrivially.

**Proof.** The edge-gradient and edge-cycle subspaces are orthogonal. The relation \(B_2^{\mathsf T}B_1^{\mathsf T}=0\) prevents the gradient sector from entering faces. The relation \(B_1B_2=0\) prevents cycle/face evolution from entering vertices. Face–core controls act only in \(C_2\oplus C_3\). These statements are independent of the face mask. \(\square\)

Consequently connectivity of the whole incidence graph does not imply full controllability on \(\mathbb R^{23}\). In particular, the uniform vertex mode is fixed, and the gradient motion cannot be independently programmed with these face controls.

## 3. Exact generated algebra

Let \(\mathfrak L\) denote the real matrix Lie algebra generated by the full mask matrices in (1). For vectors \(a,b\), write \(a\wedge b=ab^{\mathsf T}-ba^{\mathsf T}\).

**Theorem L2 (formal Lie rank).**

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak L=\mathfrak{so}(\mathcal A)\oplus\mathbb R D,
\qquad \dim\mathfrak L=91+1=92.}
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(D\) is extended by zero on \(\mathcal A\). This is a formal generated-algebra dimension, not a dimension assertion about the topological closure of an evolution subgroup.

**Constructive proof.** First consider the nine face/core coordinates. Six private-face controls are individual signed plane generators. The shared-face star is a sum of its two core contacts. A double commutator with a private face on the first core isolates the shared contact into that core; subtracting it from the shared star gives the other contact as an algebraic element. These derived matrices are Lie words, not assumed executable contact pulses.

The resulting eight contact planes connect all nine face/core coordinates. Commutators along paths generate every coordinate plane, yielding \(\mathfrak{so}(9)\), dimension 36. All signs and word scalings are provided in the certificate.

Choose five independent columns \(u_i\) of \(B_2\), spanning \(\mathcal C\). Commuting the drift with a face/core plane gives a signed \(u_i\wedge c\) for a core coordinate \(c\). Further commutators with face/core planes yield all \(5\cdot9=45\) cycle–face/core cross planes. Brackets of two such planes through a common unit core give the ten independent \(u_i\wedge u_j\) cycle planes. These 36+45+10 matrices form a 91-dimensional wedge basis on \(\mathcal A\).

The cycle–face drift lies in this basis. Subtract it from \(A_0\) to obtain \(D\), a nonzero independent algebra element. Conversely, every original mask is the sum of \(D\) and a skew operator supported on \(\mathcal A\). Operators supported on \(\mathcal A\) commute with \(D\), so this 92-dimensional space is closed under brackets and contains no further generated direction. This proves both bounds. \(\square\)

**Proof of tangent compatibility.** Put \(N=\|X_j\|^2\), \(d=X_j^{\mathsf T}X_{j+1}\). The states are not antipodal. As in v2.18,

\[

**Proof of conditional approximation.** Restrict all mask flows to \(\mathcal A\). The closure of their nonnegative-duration product semigroup is a compact subgroup of \(SO(\mathcal A)\): powers of any element have subsequences approaching the identity, so its inverse also lies in the closure. Its Lie algebra contains every restricted mask generator and therefore, by L2, all of \(\mathfrak{so}(\mathcal A)\). The closed subgroup is thus \(SO(14)\). ## 5. An exact continuous-flow obstruction on unknown gradient inputs

For \(K_-\), the vertex graph is \(K_5\) with the edge \([34]\) missing. Its Laplacian has eigenvalues \(0,3,5,5,5\). The verifier constructs exact rational spectral projectors. Hence \(D\) has one active gradient pair at frequency \(\sqrt3\), three at \(\sqrt5\), and the fixed uniform vertex mode.

**Theorem L4 (no exact nonzero-duration gradient-transparent continuous gate).** In the full native switched continuous system with unmodified lower-grade drift and nonnegative phase durations, no positive total duration can produce an operator equal to the identity on all of \(\mathcal G\).

**Proof.** Every mask restricts to the same \(D\) on \(\mathcal G\), so a sequence of total duration \(T\) restricts to \(\exp(TD)\), independent of its mask choices. Identity would require both \(\sqrt3T\) and \(\sqrt5T\) to be integer multiples of \(2\pi\). For \(T>0\), their ratio would make \(\sqrt{5/3}\) rational, a contradiction. \(\square\)

This obstructs an exact universal compiler required to leave every unknown gradient input untouched. It does not obstruct the gradient-free checkpoints (5), approximation by recurrence, added drift-refocusing controls, or a different native control model. It is not a no-go for finite Cayley products: on the gradient sector those give a product of rational Cayley phases, whose exact identity condition needs its own audit.
