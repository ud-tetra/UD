## 2. A diagnostic that passes positive refinement

For fixed nonnegative contact weights \(\kappa_e\), with \(e=(f,\tau)\), define

\[
V_\kappa(H;x)=\sum_e\kappa_e|2\alpha_fd_ex_fx_\tau|,
\qquad
\mathcal V_\kappa[a,b]=\int_a^b V_\kappa(H(t);x(t))\,dt.
\tag{3}
\]

Unit weights give the total absolute incidence receipt rate and its accumulated variation. Naming this quantity does not make it a consumed stock or physical action.

**Theorem V1 (positive coefficient refinement).** Suppose \(H=\sum_i H_i\), where \(H_i=\sum_f\alpha_{if}G_f\), \(\alpha_{if}\ge0\), and \(\sum_i\alpha_{if}=\alpha_f\). On one common field state,

\[
V_\kappa(H;x)=\sum_iV_\kappa(H_i;x).
\tag{4}
\]

The same equality holds after integrating along one common trajectory. It includes the coface-neutral cover and indexed copies \(H/m\). It does not compare different state trajectories produced by different finite schedules.

**Proof.** For each contact write \(q_e=2d_ex_fx_\tau\). Its component currents are \(\alpha_{if}q_e\), with a common sign whenever nonzero. Thus \(|\sum_i\alpha_{if}q_e|=\sum_i|\alpha_{if}q_e|\). Multiply by \(\kappa_e\), sum contacts and integrate. Negative allocation coefficients can introduce cancellation and invalidate the absolute-value equality. \(\square\)

**Theorem V2 (time partition and parameter change).** Variation is additive over successive intervals. Under an increasing \(C^1\) change \(t=\theta(s)\), with coefficients transformed as \(\widetilde\alpha_f(s)=\theta'(s)\alpha_f(\theta(s))\), its integral is unchanged. Under a constant field scaling \(x\mapsto a x\) for the same coefficient trajectory, it scales by \(a^2\).

**Proof.** Interval additivity and parameter change follow from the integral and \(\widetilde j_e=\theta'j_e\circ\theta\). Each current is quadratic in field amplitude. \(\square\)

Actual orientation-basis changes leave the signed support current unchanged: both incidence and field signs transform together. Relabelings permute contacts and their assigned weights. Covariance alone does not force one common weight if the contact set has several symmetry orbits.

For a sequence of actual finite Cayley events, one can similarly retain every native midpoint receipt \(J_{e,n}\) and sum \(\sum_{n,e}\kappa_e|J_{e,n}|\). This ledger is additive under regrouping a fixed list of actual events. Replacing that list by another schedule, or replacing accumulated absolute receipts by the absolute value of their net sum, is a different operation. V1–V2 do not restore the indexed strict-margin rule's clone invariance or resolve its equal-score competition.

## 3. What additive local costs would and would not fix

Under additional modeling axioms, consider a fixed per-contact contribution \(F_e(q)\) that is finite, nonnegative, sign-insensitive, and additive on nonnegative receipt magnitudes:

\[
F_e(-q)=F_e(q),\qquad F_e(r+s)=F_e(r)+F_e(s)
\quad(r,s\ge0).
\tag{5}
\]

**Theorem V3 (conditional weighted-absolute form).** These assumptions force \(F_e(q)=\kappa_e|q|\) for some \(\kappa_e\ge0\). They do not fix the weights.

**Proof.** On the nonnegative line, nonnegativity and additivity make \(F_e\) monotone. Let \(\kappa_e=F_e(1)\). Additivity fixes every nonnegative rational value to \(\kappa_e r\). Rational lower and upper approximations and monotonicity fix each real value. Sign-insensitivity extends the result to negative arguments. \(\square\)

The additivity and fixed-contact-function assumptions in (5) are additional cost axioms, not consequences of Q171. More general state-dependent costs are not classified by V3. Even within V3, different \(\kappa_e\), including a common multiplicative scale, pass the same refinement identity. Unit weighting is a declared counting convention. A physical action scale or reserve coupling needs a separate source.

## 4. Exact backflow control: variation differs from net transfer

Take an isolated native private face/core pair, with all other permissions off. In an orientation with \(d=1\),

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
x(u)=\left(\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2},\frac{2u}{1+u^2}\right),
\quad u\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]

This is an exact norm-one native trajectory with coefficient \(\alpha(u)=2/(1+u^2)>0\), since \(x'=\alpha Gx\). Core stock and its signed current are

\[
B(u)=\frac{4u^2}{(1+u^2)^2},\qquad
j(u)=B'(u)=\frac{8u(1-u^2)}{(1+u^2)^3}.
\tag{7}
\]

From \(u=0\) to 1, core stock rises from zero to one. From 1 to 2 it falls to \(16/25\). Therefore

\[
\int_0^2j(u)\,du=\frac{16}{25},\qquad
\int_0^2|j(u)|\,du=1+\frac9{25}=\frac{34}{25}.
\tag{8}
\]

Every partition retains additive variation when the reversal is accounted for. The absolute net receipt \(16/25\) discards the backflow contribution; it is not the accumulated variation \(34/25\). This is an exact native support example, not a dissipation model.

## 5. Initiation and budget do not follow from receipt accounting

When all core amplitudes vanish, every instantaneous current in (1) is zero, even if \(H x\ne0\) and native transport can fill a core. Consequently a readiness policy that declares all zero-current blocks inactive cannot bootstrap such a preparation. This conclusion requires that inactivity rule; it is not a no-go for every possible current-dependent policy or finite-event predictor.

In the private pair starting from \((1,0)\), an exact Cayley event of supplied model step \(t\) gives

\[
x^+(t)=\left(\frac{1-t^2/4}{1+t^2/4},
\frac{t}{1+t^2/4}\right),\qquad
J(t)=\Delta B=\frac{t^2}{(1+t^2/4)^2}.
\tag{9}
\]

On \(0<t<2\), this receipt increases strictly from zero to one. For each prescribed fraction \(0<\beta<1\), a different unique step meets \(J=\beta\):

\[
t_\beta=\frac{2\sqrt\beta}{1+\sqrt{1-\beta}}.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus a receipt-budget convention can determine a step only after a budget and stopping branch are supplied. Choosing full transfer at \(t=2\) is another possible termination policy; the source has not selected it as a universal event or action unit. Normalizing transfer by the initial local stock removes amplitude scale but still leaves the budget fraction and the branch choice.

For example, steps \(1/2,1,3/2,2\) yield receipts \(64/289,16/25,576/625,1\), respectively. Every choice preserves the original norm and signed receipts. The instantaneous initial rate is zero in all these cases. None of these support fractions has been identified with physical action.

## 6. Field return is not reserve or record renewal

**Theorem V4 (unbounded accumulated receipts at fixed support).** The original native pair permits exact field-return cycles with support norm one and zero net core transfer, but accumulated absolute midpoint receipts four per cycle. Hence cumulative gross receipts cannot be identified with the closed conserved support stock.

**Proof.** Since \(G^2=-I\), \(\operatorname{Cayley}(2G)=G\). Four successive events give

\[
(1,0)\to(0,1)\to(-1,0)\to(0,-1)\to(1,0).
\tag{11}
\]

Their exact signed core receipts are \(+1,-1,+1,-1\), so the net is zero and the gross sum is four. Repeat the field cycle \(m\) times: total support remains one while accumulated gross receipt is \(4m\). \(\square\)

This is not evidence of free physical work or dissipative renewal. It shows that circulation accounting and a finite consumable reserve are different variables. If an additional model postulates a reserve \(R\ge0\), conversion coefficient \(\gamma>0\), and a decrement \(\gamma\) times gross receipt, then after \(m\) closed cycles with no import,

\[
R_m=R_0-4\gamma m,
\qquad m\le R_0/(4\gamma).
\tag{12}
\]

Indefinite operation under that extra reserve law requires replenishment or a changed cost assignment. Neither \(R\), \(\gamma\), its coupling, nor a recharge contact is provided by (2). Returning the field also does not erase an external receipt log or reset an apparatus record. Those carriers and their outgoing/reset receipts remain separate source obligations.
