## 1. State, types and hypotheses

Take the complete filled tetrahedron with vertex set {0,1,2,3} and edge set E={01,02,03,12,13,23}, four oriented triangular faces F, and a single oriented cell τ. With standard alternating incidences and unit chain inner products,

- B₁:C₁(K,ℂ)→C₀(K,ℂ), B₂:C₂(K,ℂ)→C₁(K,ℂ), B₃:C₃(K,ℂ)→C₂(K,ℂ);
- B₁B₂=0, B₂B₃=0, and B₁†B₁+B₂B₂†=4I₆;
- a candidate *edge–face phase incidence* N:C₂→C₁ has the same nonzero support as B₂ and satisfies Nₑf=(B₂)ₑf zₑf where every |zₑf|=1;
- **B₁ and B₃ remain the original fixed topological boundaries.** Any phase gauge transformation of B₁ or B₃ must be declared and transformed consistently; a new N is not automatically a new chain boundary.

The three separate objects are: signed chain incidence N, the skew-adjoint full-chain amplitude evolution A_N, and any separately justified stochastic six-edge readout T. No Markov probabilities, physical clock or Born rule are assumed from norm conservation.

## 2. THM-534-S5 — Lower-chain phase rigidity (EXACT)

**Statement.** Within the declared same-support unit-phase class,

    B₁N=0  ⇔  N=B₂ D_F,

where D_F is an arbitrary diagonal 4×4 unitary face-phase matrix. Consequently,

    B₁†B₁+NN†=4I₆.

**Proof.** Each face column of N is supported only on the three oriented edges of its own triangular boundary. On that triangle, the kernel of B₁ restricted to those three edges is one-dimensional, generated by the ordinary signed face boundary B₂[:,f]. Therefore the column can be a cycle only if all three relative phases coincide: N[:,f]=z_f B₂[:,f]. The modulus assumption forces |z_f|=1. Collect these phases into D_F, and conversely B₁B₂D_F=0. Since D_FD_F†=I, NN†=B₂B₂† and the unit-Hodge identity gives 4I₆. QED.

**Scope:** preserving the **lower** chain identity alone does not imply NB₃=0 or full 15D edge-block no-mixing at all orders. In particular, upper-degree coupling can transmit amplitudes at fourth order; see S9.

## 3. THM-534-S6 — Full-chain flat selection and gauge uniqueness (EXACT / NO-GO)

**Statement.** Under the hypotheses of §1,

    (B₁N=0 and NB₃=0)  ⇔  N=z B₂,  z∈U(1).

All such solutions are degreewise unitary gauge-equivalent to the original chain. No non-flat seam phase survives both ordinary chain-closure identities on one filled tetrahedron.

**Proof.** By S5, N=B₂D_F. The second equation reads B₂D_FB₃=0, hence D_FB₃∈ker(B₂)=im(B₃), a one-dimensional space. Every face component of B₃ is ±1 and nonzero. Therefore D_FB₃=zB₃ forces z_f=z for all four faces. Conversely, zB₂ satisfies both identities, and the phase z can be removed by a unitary redefinition of the C₂ basis (with consistent adjacent-degree maps). QED.

**NO-GO scope:** fixed B₁ and B₃, complete filled Δ³, original incidence support, unit-modulus edge–face phases, ordinary nilpotent chain identities and unit metric. New non-flat curvature, source/defect, open carrier, nonunit metric, modified differential complex, or additional registers are different branches, not counterexamples.

**H₁ note:** the complete simplex is contractible; a flat U(1) connection on its 1-skeleton satisfying filled triangular flatness is pure gauge. Global holonomy or winding needs a nontrivial carrier, puncture, specified curvature source, or boundary condition.

## 4. THM-534-S7 — Structural seam-frustration minimum and exact Z₂ counts (EXACT, finite)

Define the **internal structural diagnostic, not a physical energy or action**,

    E(N)=||B₁N||²_F + ||NB₃||²_F ≥ 0.

**Statement.** E(N)=0 iff N=zB₂ for any U(1) phase assignment of the declared form. Restricting to Z₂ phases zₑf∈{+1,−1}, precisely 2 of 2¹²=4096 assignments have E=0, and the smallest positive E is exactly 12.

**Proof of count.** Every face contains three incidence slots (4×3=12). Lower closure requires the three phases on each face to agree, leaving 2⁴=16 assignments. Upper closure requires equal phases across each pair of face incidences at a given edge, leaving 2⁶=64 assignments. Both identities, by S6, allow only the two global choices N=±B₂. Hence class counts are (both,lower only,upper only,neither)=(2,14,62,4018).

**Proof of positive gap.** Let k count the triangular faces whose three local signs are not equal and ℓ count edges where the two incident face signs differ. For binary phases,

    E(N)=8k+4ℓ.

A nonuniform face contributes 8 to ||B₁N||², and a mismatched edge contributes 4 to ||NB₃||². If k=0 but phases are nonconstant, the four face signs define a nontrivial cut of the dual K₄ graph; every such cut has at least three edges, so ℓ≥3 and E≥12. If k≥2, E≥16. If k=1, ℓ=0 is impossible: the three other faces would be constant and pairwise edge-consistent, forcing the allegedly nonuniform face to be uniform. Thus ℓ≥1 and E≥12. One flipped edge–face incidence has (k,ℓ)=(1,1), attaining 12; flipping all three incidences of one face has (0,3), also attaining 12. QED.

**Quantization guard:** the positive gap 12 is only for binary Z₂ assignments and this chosen norm diagnostic. For unrestricted U(1), phases arbitrarily near 1 give arbitrarily small E>0; there is no U(1) positive spectral/action gap from this construction. Minimizing E is an extra **candidate selection principle**, not an admitted UD microscopic action. If postulated, its minimizers are flat and cannot source the historical THM-534 transport.

## 5. THM-534-S8 — Opposite-edge obstruction for two candidate amplitude readouts (EXACT CONDITIONAL / NO-GO)

**A. Unequal but flat lower/upper incidence weights.** Define X=B₁†B₁ and Y=B₂B₂†. They satisfy X/4+Y/4=I₆ and XY=0. Under externally supplied a,b>0, the full skew evolution's edge-sector amplitude is

    R_{a,b}(s)=cos(2√a s)(X/4)+cos(2√b s)(Y/4).

Writing c_a=cos(2√a s), c_b=cos(2√b s), one has for starting edge e:

    R_{e,e}=(c_a+c_b)/2,
    R_{f,e}=±(c_a−c_b)/4 for each of four adjacent edges f,
    R_{e_op,e}=0 for the disjoint opposite edge.

The retained edge norm is (c_a²+c_b²)/2. **Only if a conditional squared-amplitude edge readout is *separately postulated*** and this norm is nonzero, its self/opposite/per-adjacent probabilities are

    p_self=(c_a+c_b)²/[2(c_a²+c_b²)],
    p_opposite=0,
    p_cross=(c_a−c_b)²/[8(c_a²+c_b²)].

These total 1 via p_self+4p_cross=1. The readout is not UD-derived Born weighting. Nevertheless no choice of a,b,s gives the historical THM-534 **positive one-step opposite** probability 1/6 within this readout.

**B. A single nonflat face-edge incidence twist.** Let N_z=B₂+(z−1)(B₂)ₑf|e⟩⟨f| for one incidence and z∈U(1). The quantity N_zB₃ is supported only on the selected edge e, while the change in B₁†B₁+N_zN_z† couples e only to the other two edges of its selected face. The edge e_op disjoint from e is an isolated eigenvector of the entire even-power subsystem on C₁⊕C₃. Therefore

    ⟨e_op|exp(s A_{N_z})|e⟩=0   for every real s.

Here A_{N_z} is the skew full-chain generator, not a probability matrix. This exact one-entry twist can yield adjacent-edge amplitudes but not a one-step opposite-edge amplitude starting from the twisted edge, regardless of z or evolution parameter. Multiple seam defects, a different carrier/readout or separate multi-event schedule are outside this scoped obstruction.

## 6. THM-534-S9 — Upper-chain breach creates fourth-order false-closure transfer (EXACT)

Choose nonconstant D_F∈U(1)⁴, hold B₃ fixed, and let N=B₂D_F. Then B₁N=0 and K=B₁†B₁+NN†=4I₆, but v=NB₃≠0. For full skew generator A_N on C₀⊕C₁⊕C₂⊕C₃,

    (A_N²)|_{C₁} = −4I₆,
    (A_N⁴)|_{C₁} = 16I₆+vv†.

Thus for distinct edges e,f with v_e conj(v_f)≠0,

    ⟨e|exp(sA_N)|f⟩=s⁴ v_e conj(v_f)/24+O(s⁶).

This is an exact Taylor coefficient. It is **not lawful source-free edge transport** under the full chain axioms, because NB₃≠0. If B₃ is simultaneously transformed as D_F† B₃ (the proper degreewise gauge change), both chain identities are restored and the no-mixing conclusion of S1 returns. The example demonstrates why checking B₁N=0 without NB₃ is insufficient.
