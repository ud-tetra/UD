- $D_{ct}$ = defect containment
- $P_{rec}$ = recurrence potential

### Theorem X.2 — Retention Sufficiency Theorem
Strong retention requires: sufficient mechanical viability, sufficient admissibility, no catastrophic weak gate, viable transmission, and viable return.

$$A_{\min}(S) < a_0 \;\Rightarrow\; \Sigma(S) \notin \{\text{Stable}, \text{Propagate}\}$$

### Theorem X.4 — Foundational-Gate Dominance Theorem

## X.8 Threshold Authority and Decision Resolution

### Principle X.1 — Threshold Ordering Principle
A thing must first form, then deserve to remain, then survive pressure, and only then earn the right to spread.

1. Formation veto: $M_{\text{form}}$
2. Retention veto: $a_0, A_{\text{keep}}, T_{\min}, R_{\min}$

### Theorem X.16 — Threshold Veto Theorem
Formation and foundational thresholds act as non-compensable vetoes; secondary thresholds mostly govern strain and repair; propagation thresholds govern extension.

### Principle X.3 — Selection Precedence Principle

$$\text{Dissolve} \succ \text{Expel} \succ \text{Fragment} \succ \text{Repair} \succ \text{Strained} \succ \text{Propagate} \succ \text{Stable}$$

### Theorem X.17 — Precedence Resolution Theorem

### Principle X.4 — Selector Core Principle
The selector first asks whether a thing can hold, then whether it may remain, then whether it is under pressure, then whether it may extend, and finally which active truth has deepest authority.
