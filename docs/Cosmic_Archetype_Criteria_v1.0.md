**Layer B working classification | No exact structural claims | No theorem-count changes**

## 1. Definitions

Each archetype is defined by a specific **configuration of loop-spine variables** drawn from the locked algebra {T, H, P_λ, R, B_repair, dim V_λ}. The classification uses five binary or graded criteria applied to a region of the transport field at a given IFS scale.

| Criterion | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Transport dominance | τ | Whether the region is primarily propagating (high τ) or static |
| Closure retention | ρ_C | Whether R(ψ,n) stays near 1 across the region's characteristic timescale |
| Reconstructive capacity | ρ_R | Whether B_repair < B_max for typical perturbations in the region |
| Burden concentration | β | Whether the local energy density (1−λ) is concentrated or diffuse |
| Boundary stress | σ_B | Whether perturbations at eigenspace boundaries exceed Δλ_min |

## 2. The Six Archetypes

### FIELD
Transport-dominated, no closure, no reconstruction.

| τ | ρ_C | ρ_R | β | σ_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high | low | irrelevant | diffuse | low |

A region where states propagate under T but do not return to themselves. Retention R(ψ,n) decays toward R(∞). No definite quantum numbers persist. The region is a superposition across eigenspaces with no single V_λ dominant.

*What kind of pattern: transport without identity — flow that hasn't yet become anything.*

---

### SINK
Convergence-dominated, closure collapsing to V₁.

| τ | ρ_C | ρ_R | β | σ_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| inward | failing | exceeded | extreme | irrelevant |

A region where the local burden concentration β exceeds all reconstruction thresholds. All eigenspace content is forced toward V₁ (the zero-energy vacuum mode). T^n → P₁ convergence (THM-LS10) dominates. Closure loops that enter cannot maintain identity — their R decays and their eigenspace content is absorbed.

*What kind of pattern: the cost ordering's endpoint — everything falls toward zero burden.*

---

### EMITTER
Reconstruction failing, burden converting to transport.

| τ | ρ_C | ρ_R | β | σ_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| outward | partial | failing | decreasing | moderate |

A region where a previously reconstructive state (L_R) can no longer sustain B_repair < B_max. The excess eigenspace energy is shed as outgoing L_T modes. The state transitions from L_R toward L_T, releasing the difference in burden as radiation. ρ_R is actively decreasing over the characteristic timescale.

*What kind of pattern: closure breaking open — stored burden escaping as transport.*

---

### CLUSTER
Multiple independent L_R states sharing an L_T medium.

| τ | ρ_C | ρ_R | β | σ_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shared | per-member | per-member | localized per member | low between members |

A region containing multiple independently reconstructive packets, each maintaining its own eigenspace identity, coupled through a shared transport field (typically V₁-dominated at the coupling scale). The cluster as a whole is NOT a closure loop — it is a collection. Individual members have ρ_C and ρ_R; the collective has only τ.

*What kind of pattern: separate identities sharing a transport bath — the social structure of matter.*

---

### CIRCULATION
Macro-scale closure — transport that returns.

| τ | ρ_C | ρ_R | β | σ_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rotational | high | varies by zone | concentrated in arms/core | low globally |

A region where the transport field itself forms a large-scale closure loop: the state approximately returns after a characteristic period. ρ_C is high at the scale of the full circulation. Within the circulation, different zones may have different ρ_R (spiral arms: high ρ_R, inter-arm regions: low ρ_R). The circulation is an L_C mode at the macro IFS level.

*What kind of pattern: transport that remembers where it started — identity at scale.*

---

### RUPTURE
Eigenspace boundary failure under perturbation.

| τ | ρ_C | ρ_R | β | σ_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chaotic | breaking | exceeded locally | redistributing | high |

A region where the perturbation at eigenspace boundaries exceeds the spectral gap energy. The state tears along the boundary between V_λ and V_μ. Fragments retain partial eigenspace content but the original closure is destroyed. σ_B > (Δλ_min)² is the defining condition. The result is fragmentation: multiple smaller L_R or L_T states replacing one larger structure.

*What kind of pattern: the crack — where the spectral gap becomes a fault line.*

## 3. Exact Ingredients vs Interpretive Mapping

**Locked loop-spine ingredients used (Layer A):**
- T eigenvalues {1, 1/2, 1/3} and eigenspaces V_λ
- R(ψ,n) retention fidelity (THM-LS1)
- B_repair energy-projection functional (THM-LS2)
- B = 0 in degenerate sectors (THM-LS3)
- T^n → P₁ convergence at rate (1/2)^n (THM-LS10)
- Repair cost ordering ρ(V_μ) = 1−μ (THM-LS7)
- Spectral gap Δλ_min = 1/6 (THM-LS6)

**Interpretive cosmology mapping (Layer B):**
- "Field = nebula" — identification of L_T with diffuse astrophysical media
- "Sink = black hole" — identification of V₁ convergence with gravitational collapse
- "Emitter = pulsar/supernova" — identification of B_repair failure with stellar radiation
- "Cluster = star cluster" — identification of distributed L_R with gravitational binding
- "Circulation = spiral galaxy" — identification of macro L_C with galactic rotation
- "Rupture = meteorite fragmentation" — identification of boundary failure with fracture

Every astrophysical identification is Layer B. The loop-spine criteria (τ, ρ_C, ρ_R, β, σ_B) are defined from Layer A ingredients but their application to specific cosmic objects is interpretive.

## Cosmology Grammar Summary

Six patterns exhaust the visible cosmic vocabulary: fields that flow without identity, sinks where everything converges to zero burden, emitters where closure breaks and burden escapes, clusters where separate identities share transport, circulations where transport returns at scale, and ruptures where the spectral gap becomes a crack. Each pattern is a specific configuration of the same five loop-spine variables — transport dominance, closure retention, reconstructive capacity, burden concentration, and boundary stress — applied at a given IFS scale. The grammar is the loop spine. The cosmos is its expression.
