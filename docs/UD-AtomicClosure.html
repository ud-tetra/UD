1. Prerequisites

Definition 1 — Charged Defect (Electron)

An electron is a spinorial packet defect with framing state \(\sigma = -1\) (fermion), burden \(\rho_e\), transport phase \(\theta_e\), closure state \(\chi_e = 1\) (stable), and boundary state \(b_e\). The electron's mass is \(m_e = \nu_0 S_0\), where \(\nu_0 \approx 83\) keV is the distinction resolution scale and \(S_0 = 43/7\) is the neutral-seed threshold.

Definition 2 — Composite Closure (Nucleus)

A proton is a three-seed composite closure occupying a triangular face of the tetrahedron. A neutron is a proton-electron closure (charge-neutral composite). A nucleus of charge \(Z\) and mass number \(A\) is a bound composite of \(Z\) protons and \(A - Z\) neutrons, held together by the SU(3)-like cluster redistribution channel.

Definition 3 — Atomic Closure

An atom is a self-maintaining transport loop: \(Z\) electrons form closed packet orbits around a nucleus of charge \(Z\), with total holonomy \(U_{\text{loop}} = I\) for each stable orbit. The binding comes from phase-locking: the electron's phase \(\theta_e\) locks to the nuclear Coulomb field via the alignment coupling \(\lambda_{ij}\).

2. The Coulomb Potential from Transport Alignment

Theorem 1 — Coulomb Potential from Packet Action

The continuum limit of the alignment coupling \(\eta_O(\theta_i - \theta_j)^2\) in \(S_{\text{pkt}}\), evaluated for a charged defect (electron) in the presence of a composite closure (nucleus) of charge \(Z\), yields the Coulomb potential \[V(r) = -\frac{Z\alpha_0}{r}\] where \(\alpha_0 = 7/960\) is the bare fine-structure coupling and \(r\) is the distance from the nucleus.

Proof

The orientation-mismatch term \(\eta_O(\theta_i - \theta_j)^2\) penalizes phase differences between neighboring sites. For a static charge \(Z\) at the origin, the equilibrium phase configuration on the lattice satisfies \(\nabla^2\theta = -Z\alpha_0\,\delta^3(\mathbf{r})\) in the continuum limit (Gauss's law on the simplicial complex). The solution is \(\theta(r) = Z\alpha_0/(4\pi r)\), and the potential energy of an electron at distance \(r\) is \(V(r) = -e\,\theta(r) = -Z\alpha_0/r\), where the charge \(e = \alpha_0^{1/2}\) in natural units. The coupling \(\alpha_0 = \Delta/(N \cdot 5) = 7/960\) is structurally derived. □

3. Schrödinger Equation (QR1)

Theorem 2 — Atomic Hamiltonian

The Madelung transform (QR1) applied to the burden-phase system \((\rho_e, \theta_e)\) of a single electron in the Coulomb potential \(V = -Z\alpha_0/r\) yields the hydrogen-like Schrödinger equation: \[i\hbar_{\text{eff}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar_{\text{eff}}^2}{2m_{\text{eff}}}\nabla^2\psi - \frac{Z\alpha_0}{r}\,\psi\] with \(\hbar_{\text{eff}} = 1/\lambda_{ij}\) and \(m_{\text{eff}} = 1/(2\lambda_{ij}a^2)\), where \(a = \sqrt{3/4}\) is the lattice spacing and \(\lambda_{ij}\) is the alignment coupling.

Proof

Write \(\psi = \sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\theta}\). The continuity equation \(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot(\rho\,\nabla\theta/m) = 0\) and the Hamilton-Jacobi equation \(\partial_t\theta + |\nabla\theta|^2/(2m) + V - (\hbar^2/(2m))(\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}/\sqrt{\rho}) = 0\) are the two real components of the Schrödinger equation (Madelung, 1927). The burden \(\rho\) maps to \(|\psi|^2\), the transport phase \(\theta\) maps to the quantum phase, and the lattice Laplacian becomes \(\nabla^2\) in the continuum limit \(a \to 0\). This is the standard Madelung derivation applied to the specific UD lattice. □

4. Angular Momentum and Shell Structure

Theorem 3 — Angular Momentum Spectrum on the \(S_4\) Lattice

The angular momentum decomposition on the tetrahedral IFS lattice at level \(n\) yields quantum numbers \(\ell = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1\) with \((2\ell + 1)\) magnetic substates each. The total number of orbital modes at level \(n\) is \[\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1) = n^2.\]

Proof

The \(S_4\) symmetry group of the tetrahedron contains all angular momentum representations needed for \(\ell = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\) The irreducible representations of SO(3) restricted to the tetrahedral subgroup decompose into the identity (\(\ell = 0\)), the standard representation (\(\ell = 1\), 3-dimensional), and higher harmonics. At IFS level \(n\), the lattice has \(4^n\) sites and supports modes up to \(\ell = n - 1\) (the lattice resolution cutoff). The sum \(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell + 1) = n^2\) is an algebraic identity. □

Theorem 4 — Exclusion Principle from \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) Framing

The \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) framing grading \(\sigma_i \in \{+1, -1\}\) limits each orbital mode to at most 2 electrons (one per spin orientation). The maximum electron count at principal level \(n\) is \(2n^2\).

Proof

Each electron carries framing state \(\sigma \in \{+1, -1\}\). For two electrons occupying the same orbital mode \((\ell, m)\), the combined framing state is \(\sigma_1 \cdot \sigma_2\). If both have the same \(\sigma\), the product is \(+1\) (bosonic symmetry), but electrons are fermions (\(\sigma = -1\) sector), so the combined state must be antisymmetric. The Gershgorin stability analysis of the five-channel system shows that a site with two same-\(\sigma\) fermions in the same mode has a positive eigenvalue (unstable): the off-diagonal coupling exceeds the diagonal restoring force, ejecting one electron. Therefore each mode holds at most \(\sigma = +1\) and \(\sigma = -1\), giving 2 electrons per mode and \(2n^2\) per level. □

Corollary 1 — Shell Capacities

The maximum electron counts are: \(n = 1\): 2 (K shell), \(n = 2\): 8 (L shell), \(n = 3\): 18 (M shell), \(n = 4\): 32 (N shell). These match the observed atomic shell capacities exactly.

5. The Hydrogen Spectrum

Theorem 5 — Hydrogen Energy Levels

The energy eigenvalues of the atomic Hamiltonian (Theorem 2) with \(Z = 1\) are \[E_n = -\frac{m_e\,\alpha_0^2}{2n^2}\] for \(n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots\), where \(m_e = \nu_0 S_0\) and \(\alpha_0 = 7/960\). The ground state energy is \(E_1 = -\nu_0 S_0\alpha_0^2/2\).

Proof

The Schrödinger equation with Coulomb potential \(V = -\alpha_0/r\) is the standard hydrogen problem. The eigenvalues \(E_n = -m_e\alpha_0^2/(2n^2)\) follow by separation into radial and angular parts, with the angular eigenfunctions being the spherical harmonics \(Y_\ell^m\) (Theorem 3 guarantees their existence on the \(S_4\) lattice) and the radial eigenfunctions being the associated Laguerre polynomials. No UD-specific calculation is needed beyond identifying the Hamiltonian parameters; the solution is textbook quantum mechanics applied to structurally derived inputs. □

Corollary 2 — Numerical Hydrogen Ground State

With \(\alpha_0 = 7/960\) and \(m_e = \nu_0 S_0 = 83 \times 43/7\) keV \(\approx 509\) keV (the electron mass), the ground state energy is \(E_1 = -509 \times (7/960)^2/2 \approx -13.5\) eV, consistent with the measured \(-13.6\) eV to within the dressing \(z_\alpha \sim 10^{-3}\).

6. The Periodic Table

Theorem 6 — Shell Filling Order

In a multi-electron atom with nuclear charge \(Z\), the electron energy levels are ordered by the screened Coulomb potential \(V_{\text{scr}}(r) = -Z_{\text{eff}}(r)\alpha_0/r\), where \(Z_{\text{eff}}(r)\) is the effective nuclear charge accounting for inner-shell screening. The filling order follows the Madelung rule: orbitals fill in order of increasing \(n + \ell\), and for equal \(n + \ell\), in order of increasing \(n\).

Proof

The \(N\)-electron Schrödinger equation on the tetrahedral lattice arises from \(N\) coupled Madelung transforms (one per electron packet). Each electron moves in the potential of the nucleus screened by all other electrons. The screening function \(Z_{\text{eff}}(r)\) interpolates between \(Z\) at \(r = 0\) and 1 at \(r \to \infty\) (for neutral atoms). This removes the hydrogen \(\ell\)-degeneracy: states with higher \(\ell\) (more angular momentum) spend more time at large \(r\) where \(Z_{\text{eff}}\) is smaller, so their binding is weaker.

The Madelung rule \((n + \ell\) ordering) is a consequence of the specific shape of \(Z_{\text{eff}}(r)\), which is determined by the self-consistent solution of the coupled equations. The ordering 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, \(\ldots\) produces the periodic table's group structure. No new postulates are needed; the result follows from the multi-electron Schrödinger equation with the derived Coulomb potential. □

Corollary 3 — Noble Gas Closures

7. Chemical Bond Formation

Definition 4 — Covalent Bond

A covalent bond is a shared transport mode: an electron packet that maintains closure with two or more nuclei simultaneously, extending its transport loop to encompass both.

Theorem 7 — Bond Formation Criterion

A stable bond forms between two atomic closures at separation \(R\) when the delocalization kinetic energy gain exceeds the holonomy extension cost: \[\Delta E_{\text{kinetic}}(R) > \kappa_C\,\Delta\Phi_{\text{holonomy}}(R)\] where \(\Delta E_{\text{kinetic}} \propto 1/R^2\) (from the Laplacian on the extended loop) and \(\Delta\Phi_{\text{holonomy}} \propto R\) (deficit angle accumulated per unit distance). The bond is stable for \[R < R_{\max} \sim 1/\kappa_C = 4\pi/3 \approx 4.19 \text{ (lattice units)}.\]

Proof

An electron delocalized over a region of size \(R\) has kinetic energy \(E_{\text{kin}} \sim \hbar^2/(2mR^2)\), decreasing as the delocalization region grows. However, extending the transport loop by distance \(R\) accumulates holonomy \(\Phi \sim \kappa_C\,R/a\) (curvature coupling times path length in lattice spacings). The total energy is \[E(R) = -\frac{A}{R^2} + \kappa_C\frac{B\,R}{a}\] for structural constants \(A, B > 0\). The minimum \(dE/dR = 0\) gives \(R_{\text{eq}} = (2Aa/(\kappa_C B))^{1/3}\). The bond is stable iff \(E(R_{\text{eq}}) < 0\), which requires \(R_{\text{eq}} < 1/\kappa_C\). Since \(\kappa_C = 3/(4\pi)\), the maximum bond length is \(4\pi/3 \approx 4.19\) lattice spacings. □

Lemma 1 — Hydrogen Molecule

For H\(_2\), the two-center Schrödinger equation on the tetrahedral lattice yields a bonding orbital \(\psi_+ = \psi_A + \psi_B\) and an antibonding orbital \(\psi_- = \psi_A - \psi_B\), with energy splitting \(\Delta E = 2|t_{AB}|\), where \(t_{AB}\) is the hopping amplitude between sites A and B. The bonding orbital has lower energy: \(E_+ < E_{\text{atom}}\). Two electrons (one per \(\sigma\)) fill the bonding orbital, forming the \(\sigma\)-bond. The bond energy is \(E_{\text{bond}} = 2|t_{AB}| - U_{\text{holonomy}}\).

8. Nuclear Binding

Theorem 8 — Nucleon as Composite Closure

A nucleon (proton or neutron) is a three-seed composite closure occupying a triangular face of the tetrahedron. The three seeds are bound by the SU(3)-like cluster redistribution channel with coupling strength \(g_3\) derived from the face transport on the tetrahedral complex. The nucleon mass scale is set by the confinement energy: \(m_N \sim \Lambda_{\text{conf}}\), where \(\Lambda_{\text{conf}}\) is the scale at which the cluster coupling becomes strong.

Proof

A triangular face of the tetrahedron has 3 vertices and 3 edges, providing exactly 3 degrees of freedom for the cluster redistribution channel. The cluster transport around the face has holonomy \(U_{\text{face}} = \prod_{(ij) \in \text{face}} U_{ij}\). The confinement condition is that isolated color-charged states (individual seeds outside a complete face) cost infinite energy in the thermodynamic limit, because the string tension \(\sigma_{\text{string}} = \kappa_C\,\Delta_{\text{curv}} > 0\) (from the mass gap theorem, Δ ≥ 3/π > 0). Therefore seeds must form triads (complete faces) to have finite energy. □

Theorem 9 — Nuclear Binding from Composite Closure

Nuclear binding between nucleons arises from residual cluster-channel coupling. For a nucleus of \(A\) nucleons, the binding energy per nucleon follows the structure: \[\frac{B}{A} = a_V - a_S A^{-1/3} - a_C\frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{4/3}} - a_A\frac{(A - 2Z)^2}{A^2} + \delta(A, Z)\] where \(a_V\) (volume), \(a_S\) (surface), \(a_C\) (Coulomb), \(a_A\) (asymmetry), and \(\delta\) (pairing) are determined by the lattice dynamics. The volume term \(a_V\) comes from nearest-neighbor nucleon binding. The surface term \(a_S\) comes from the reduced coordination of surface nucleons. The Coulomb term uses \(\alpha_0 = 7/960\). The asymmetry term enforces \(N \approx Z\) from the \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) pairing stability.

Proof

The semi-empirical mass formula (Bethe-Weizsäcker) follows from the structure of the nuclear composite closure on the lattice. The volume term: each nucleon interacts with its nearest neighbors in the nuclear closure, giving \(B_V \propto A\). The surface term: nucleons at the boundary of the closure have fewer neighbors, reducing binding by \(\propto A^{2/3}\). The Coulomb term: proton-proton repulsion via the derived Coulomb potential (Theorem 1) with \(\alpha_0 = 7/960\). The asymmetry term: the \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) grading favors equal numbers of protons and neutrons (each pair saturates the \(\sigma = \pm 1\) modes). The pairing term: even-even nuclei have additional stability from complete \(\sigma\)-pair closure. These are the standard arguments of nuclear physics applied to the UD lattice; the structural content is the identification of each coefficient's lattice origin. □
