## Spectral proof of the average

Copy parity gives two independent one-packet propagators Uplus and Uminus, generated by A+B and A−B. For each invariant sector and its seed vs:

```text
y_s(s) = [Uplus(s) - Uminus(s)] vs / 2
||y_s(s)||^2
  = ||vs||^2 / 2
    - vs^T Uplus(s)^T Uminus(s) vs / 2.
```

Both propagators preserve norm. The cross term averages to zero when their spectra in that sector are disjoint. Repeated eigenvalues inside a parity block cause no difficulty: they are grouped before taking the average.

In the sign sector, the two characteristic polynomials are p(b,z) and p(−b,z). In the standard sector, they are p(a,z) squared and p(−a,z) squared, with:

```text
p(t,z) = z^4 + (8 + 6t + 3t^2) z^2 + (3t + 4)^2
p(t,z) - p(-t,z) = 12t (z^2 + 4)
resultant_z[p(t,z), p(-t,z)] = 186624 t^8.
```

The resultant is nonzero for every nonzero real t. Thus opposite copy parities have no common eigenvalue in an active sector, including the special t = plus or minus 4/3 zero-frequency branches. Equivalently, any putative shared root would satisfy z squared = −4, but substitution into p gives −3t squared. The zero-frequency branch in one parity has no matching zero branch in the other.

Finite skew matrices are orthogonally reducible to rotations and stationary directions. Their complex spectral propagators contain finitely many frequency terms. Disjoint parity spectra remove every zero-frequency cross term, so its continuous-time average vanishes exactly. In an inactive sector B is zero and Uplus = Uminus, making y identically zero. Orthogonality then sums the three sector stocks and proves the displayed mean.

## Recurrence prevents a lasting positive stock floor

The following statement applies to every finite, autonomous, constant real skew generator G, including the previously supplied contact stars. Let all designated recipients be empty at launch. Then:

```text
There are arbitrarily late times s_n with exp(G s_n) arbitrarily close to I.
Hence S_recipients(s_n) tends to 0.
Therefore liminf as s tends to infinity of S_recipients(s) = 0.
```

Proof: orthogonal block reduction expresses exp(Gs) as finitely many planar rotations with frequencies omega_j and stationary identity blocks. Simultaneous approximation of the phases gives times at which every rotation is arbitrarily close to the identity. For example, a pigeonhole argument applied to q omega_j/(2 pi) yields a positive integer q no larger than Q to the power m with all phase errors at most 1/Q, where m is the number of rotation blocks. This gives an operator return error at most 2 pi/Q. If the approximating q values stay bounded as Q grows, one q is an exact common period, whose multiples give arbitrarily late returns; otherwise an unbounded subsequence supplies them. This existence proof imposes no native discrete clock or physical cadence.

The return bound implies recipient stock no larger than (2 pi/Q) squared times total launch stock at those return times. The bound is mathematical and may be very loose. No short or observable return period is claimed.

If recruitment occurs at any time s_star with positive recipient stock, recurrent returns followed by the fixed additional time s_star reproduce that positive stock arbitrarily late. Thus the upper limit is positive and the stock cannot converge. It can have the positive Cesaro mean just derived while repeatedly approaching zero.

This theorem does not require exact emptying at a finite time. It rules out an eventually uniform positive lower stock bound, not the persistence of the full recurrent trajectory or of a supplied contact graph.
